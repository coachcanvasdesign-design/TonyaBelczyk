Clinical Therapies

Therapies

We provide individualized, evidence-based therapy across the following areas:

01 — Service Category

Speech

When speech is difficult to understand, it can make everyday communication challenging. We provide evidence-based assessment and therapy for speech sound disorders, helping children improve clarity, confidence, and connection.

Articulation therapy helps children build the skills to produce speech sounds clearly and consistently. We start by teaching kids how each sound is physically formed, then guide them through practice at increasing levels of difficulty—moving from single sounds, to syllables and words, to full sentences, and finally to natural conversation. Sessions are structured yet fun, helping children steadily grow more confident and easier to understand in everyday life.

Phonological therapy helps children understand and apply the sound patterns that make up language. Some children rely on predictable patterns that affect groups of sounds rather than just one, which can make their speech harder for others to follow. Through intervention, children learn to recognize and produce sounds more accurately, while also building key pre-literacy skills like sound awareness and letter-sound connections—laying the groundwork for future reading and spelling success.

02 — Service Category

Language

Strong language skills are essential for learning, building relationships, and communicating with others. We provide individualized, evidence-based therapy to help children strengthen their understanding and use of language.

Expressive language therapy helps children develop the words, sentences, and grammar they need to share their thoughts and ideas effectively. This includes expanding vocabulary, learning to combine words into clear and complete sentences, and mastering grammar skills—like verb tenses, plurals, and pronouns—that make speech easier to understand. Therapy offers structured practice along with real-life opportunities to use language meaningfully, helping children become more confident communicators at home, at school, and with peers.

Receptive language (understanding language) refers to a child's ability to make sense of what they hear. Some children struggle with this, showing up in areas like following directions, understanding vocabulary, answering questions, comprehending stories, making inferences, and processing more complex language. Building strong comprehension skills helps children learn, participate, and engage more successfully at school and in everyday life.

Social communication (also called pragmatics) refers to the practical skills children use to connect and interact with others—skills that go beyond just knowing words and sentences. Some children need support understanding social situations, seeing things from another person's perspective, taking part in back-and-forth conversation, recognizing and repairing communication breakdowns, or navigating friendships. Therapy targets these real-world skills, helping children build stronger relationships and participate confidently in social settings.

03 — Additional Supports

Additional Supports

Augmentative & Alternative Communication

AAC

Communication is about so much more than speech alone. Every child deserves a way to express their thoughts, feelings, and personality. Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) includes a range of tools and strategies that support children who have difficulty communicating through speech alone—from picture systems and communication boards to speech-generating devices and other personalized tools. AAC isn't a last resort—it's a pathway to connection, giving children a voice to participate fully in their everyday lives.

Foundational Learning

Literacy

Literacy and language skills go hand in hand. Many children benefit from support with the language foundations that underlie reading comprehension, written expression, curriculum learning, and overall academic success. Intervention may target vocabulary, narrative skills, inferencing, comprehension monitoring, metalinguistic awareness, and other higher-level language skills children need to succeed in the classroom.
